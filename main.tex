\begin{abstract}
Stochastic gradient descent estimates gradients afresh. Transported SGD
(TSGD) instead estimates gradient changes and uses them to transport an
initial estimate along the optimization trajectory. This known recursion
is a fundamental estimation mechanism, alongside fresh estimation and
momentum averaging, and is already embedded in many modern optimizers.
Its advantage arises when stochastic gradient differences are accurate
even though individual gradients are noisy. We explain this distinction
through centered similarity, which measures noise variation rather than
pointwise noise magnitude. Exact examples and a controlled numerical
experiment reveal regimes favoring transport or fresh gradients. A
finite-horizon convergence guarantee separates optimization, transport,
and initialization contributions, while exact quadratic dynamics show
how inherited and accumulated errors can prevent stationarity. These
results provide a unified view of transport and its combination with
refreshing and averaging in modern stochastic optimization.
\end{abstract}
