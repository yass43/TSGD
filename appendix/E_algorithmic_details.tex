\subsection{Additional Estimator Identities and Sampling Details}
\label{app:algorithmic-details}

We expand the relationships in Table~\ref{tab:transport-building-blocks}.
Throughout, $H_{k+1}$ is a fresh unbiased estimate of
$\nabla f(x_{k+1})$, possibly a minibatch average or an exact gradient,
and $D_{k+1}$ estimates
$\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_k)$. Unless stated otherwise,
the two endpoints of a stochastic difference use the same fresh sample
or minibatch. These are identities for the gradient estimators; each
method has its own stepsizes, output rules, and iterate update.

\subsubsection{Periodic Restarts: SARAH and SPIDER}
\label{subsec:periodic-restarts}

SARAH initializes an inner loop with a full gradient and then applies
$g_{k+1}=g_k+D_{k+1}$ using stochastic gradient differences
\citep{nguyen2017sarah,nguyen2017sarah_nonconvex}. SARAH-IN is therefore
a transport phase with exact initialization. Inexact SARAH replaces
the full gradient by a sufficiently accurate minibatch estimate
\citep{nguyen2021inexact}. SPIDER uses the same recursive estimator
between periodic full-gradient or large-batch recomputations
\citep{fang2018spider}; normalized or adaptive iterate updates are
additional features of that method.

For a transport phase starting at iteration $s$, conditional
unbiasedness gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:restart-error-window}
E_k=E_s+\sum_{t=s}^{k-1}\mathbb E[\|d_{t+1}\|^2].
\end{equation}
Indeed, $e_{t+1}=e_t+d_{t+1}$ and
$\mathbb E[\langle e_t,d_{t+1}\rangle]=0$ give
$E_{t+1}=E_t+\mathbb E[\|d_{t+1}\|^2]$, which telescopes over the
phase. At a restart, replacing the estimator by an exact gradient sets
$E_s=0$; a fresh independent batch of size $B$ gives
$E_s\leq\sigma^2/B$ under BV. Tracking errors then accumulate only
within the new phase. Shorter phases limit this accumulation but
require more frequent recomputations. A restart corrects the estimate
at the current point; it does not undo the effect of earlier errors
on the iterates.

\subsubsection{Random Restarts: Geom-SARAH}
\label{subsec:random-restarts}

Geom-SARAH retains the SARAH recursion but draws the inner-loop length
from a geometric distribution \citep{horvath2022adaptivity}. Each epoch
starts with a fresh batch estimate and passes its final point to the
next epoch. Batch sizes and expected epoch lengths may vary between
epochs. If $N_j$ is the number of inner steps in epoch $j$, write,
for $0<p_j\leq1$,
\[
\mathbb P(N_j=n)=p_j(1-p_j)^n,\quad n\geq0,
\qquad \mathbb E[N_j]=\frac{1-p_j}{p_j}.
\]
The memoryless property corresponds to a constant stopping probability
within an epoch. With initialization batch $B_j$ and transport batch
$b_j$, the expected number of pointwise gradient evaluations is at most
$B_j+2b_j\mathbb E[N_j]$. Longer phases spread the initialization cost
over more steps but allow errors to accumulate for longer. The geometric
law controls the expected duration without imposing a deterministic
maximum. Geom-SARAH couples this randomization with batch-size
schedules in its convergence analysis.

\subsubsection{Probabilistic Refreshing: PAGE and MARINA}
\label{subsec:probabilistic-refreshing}

For a fixed refresh probability $p$, PAGE's estimator is
\citep{li2021page}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:page-refresh-transport}
g_{k+1}=c_{k+1}H_{k+1}
       +(1-c_{k+1})(g_k+D_{k+1}),
\qquad c_{k+1}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p).
\end{equation}
The decision is independent of the history and the new samples. The
refresh branch uses a fresh batch, and the transport branch typically
uses a smaller batch of coupled differences. Define
\[
q_k=\mathbb E[\|d_{k+1}\|^2],\qquad
v_{k+1}=\mathbb E[\|H_{k+1}-\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^2].
\]
Conditioning on the branch and using conditional unbiasedness of the
transport increment yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:refresh-error-recursion}
E_{k+1}=(1-p)(E_k+q_k)+p\,v_{k+1}.
\end{equation}
There is no cross term between the two branches because exactly one
is selected. Within the transport branch, the cross term between
$e_k$ and $d_{k+1}$ vanishes by conditional unbiasedness.
Each transport error survives only until the next refresh. An exact
refresh has $v_{k+1}=0$; a stochastic refresh replaces the previous
error by its own batch error. The boundary $p=0$ recovers TSGD, while
$p=1$ gives minibatch SGD. For $0<p<1$, refreshing mitigates persistent
initialization error and accumulated transport errors.

MARINA uses the same probabilistic choice in distributed optimization
\citep{gorbunov2021marina}. For $f=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f_i$, the refresh branch
computes $H_{k+1}=\nabla f(x_{k+1})$ and the transport branch uses
compressed local gradient differences:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:marina-increment}
D_{k+1}^{\mathcal Q}
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathcal Q_{i,k+1}\!\left(
\nabla f_i(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_i(x_k)\right).
\end{equation}
Unbiased compression makes this increment conditionally unbiased,
although the full gradient estimator need not be conditionally unbiased.
Refreshing removes
accumulated compression error; transporting communicates compressed
changes. Thus increment noise can arise from compression as well as
stochastic gradient sampling. Verifying a similarity bound for the
compressed increment requires the corresponding compression and
local-smoothness assumptions; unbiased compression alone only
establishes the conditional mean.

\subsubsection{Mixing Fresh and Transported Estimates: MVR and STORM}
\label{subsec:mixing-estimates}

The momentum variance-reduction recursion used by STORM combines a
fresh estimate with a transported estimate at every iteration
\citep{cutkosky2019momentum}. For fresh weight $\alpha\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mvr-mixture}
\begin{aligned}
g_{k+1}
&=(1-\alpha)(g_k+D_{k+1})+\alpha H_{k+1}\\
&=h(x_{k+1},\xi_{k+1})
 +(1-\alpha)\bigl(g_k-h(x_k,\xi_{k+1})\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second equality uses the same fresh sample $\xi_{k+1}$ in
$H_{k+1}$ and $D_{k+1}$. STORM chooses an iteration-dependent weight
linked to its stepsize; the fixed-weight notation exposes the estimator
identity. Subtracting the true gradient gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mvr-error-recursion}
e_{k+1}=(1-\alpha)e_k+(1-\alpha)d_{k+1}
 +\alpha\bigl(H_{k+1}-\nabla f(x_{k+1})\bigr).
\end{equation}
Mixing damps inherited error while introducing transport and
fresh-gradient noise. A larger $\alpha$ strengthens the correction but
places more weight on pointwise noise; a smaller $\alpha$ relies more
on accurate changes. The boundaries $\alpha=0$ and $\alpha=1$ give
TSGD and SGD, respectively.

For a deterministic weight, conditional unbiasedness eliminates cross
terms with $e_k$, so the precise mean-square relation is
\[
\begin{split}
E_{k+1}={}&(1-\alpha)^2(E_k+q_k)+\alpha^2v_{k+1}\\
&+2\alpha(1-\alpha)\mathbb E\!\left[
\left\langle d_{k+1},H_{k+1}-\nabla f(x_{k+1})\right\rangle
\right].
\end{split}
\]
Unlike PAGE's mutually exclusive branches, the two new noises occur
together and share a sample. Their covariance generally cannot be
discarded. If weights depend on the history, the analogous equality
holds conditionally, with the weights retained inside expectations
after averaging over that history.

\subsubsection{Transport Corrections within Momentum: MARS and VENUS}
\label{subsec:mars-transport}

Before clipping, MARS scales the gradient-difference correction
within momentum \citep{yuan2025mars}. Write $\beta=1-\beta_1$ for
the fresh-gradient weight, where $\beta_1$ is its original momentum
coefficient. The estimator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mars-estimator}
g_{k+1}=(1-\beta)(g_k+\gamma D_{k+1})+\beta H_{k+1}.
\end{equation}
The difference uses the same sample at both endpoints. Setting
$\gamma=0$ yields MSGD's exponential moving average; $\gamma=1$
yields MVR with fresh weight $\beta$. For $\gamma\in[0,1]$, the
VENUS manuscript gives the convex interpolation
\citep{venus2026draft}
\[
g_{k+1}^{\mathrm{MARS}}
=(1-\gamma)g_{k+1}^{\mathrm{MSGD}}
 +\gamma g_{k+1}^{\mathrm{MVR}},
\]
where the two candidates share the incoming estimate, sample, and
fresh weight $\beta$. The expanded identity gives pure transport at
$(\beta,\gamma)=(0,1)$, although MARS's original rescaled correction
is undefined at $\beta=0$.

For $\gamma\ne1$, the conditional mean of $\gamma D_{k+1}$ is
$\gamma(\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_k))$, so
Assumption~\ref{ass:unbiased-transport} generally fails.
Clipping and preconditioning further change the algorithm. A difference
using a cached gradient can violate both conditional unbiasedness and
similarity (Assumptions~\ref{ass:unbiased-transport}--\ref{ass:centered-similarity}).

VENUS further separates the samples used by the MSGD and MVR branches
\citep{venus2026draft}. It preserves shared sampling within each
transported difference but assigns part of the fresh contribution to
an independent batch. This changes the covariance between refreshing
and transporting as well as their weights. The estimator design
therefore depends on sample coupling, not only on the coefficients
in a linear combination.

\subsubsection{Pure Transport as an Established Special Case}

The transport mechanism has also been studied independently of
refreshing, including SARAH-IN and iSARAH-IN
\citep{nguyen2017sarah_nonconvex,nguyen2021inexact} and PAGE-NS
\citep{sokolov2022nonconvex}. The $p=0$ boundary of PAGE is the same
recursion \citep{li2021page}. The recursive-gradient (RG) estimator
used within the inexact composite gradient method (I-CGM) also reduces
to pure transport at its $\beta=0$ boundary \citep{jiang2026nonconvex}.
Here $\beta$ is the RG mixing parameter, distinct from MARS's fresh
weight. The shared recursion does not imply identical iterate updates
or convergence assumptions.
