\section{Oracle Complexity}
\label{app:oracle-complexity}

\subsection{Initialization and Evaluation Costs}
\label{app:initialization-cost}

Averaging $B_0$ independent unbiased gradients at $x_0$, independently
of subsequent samples, gives $E_0\leq\sigma_0^2/B_0$, where $\sigma_0^2$
bounds the variance at $x_0$. Thus
$B_0\geq\max\{1,\lceil3\sigma_0^2/\varepsilon^2\rceil\}$
suffices for Corollary~\ref{cor:tsgd-iteration-complexity}.
For $K$ updates, SGD uses $K$ evaluations and shared-sample TSGD uses
$B_0+2(K-1)$; the last transport correction is unused.
Combining these costs with the iteration bounds, under BV,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:comparison-oracle-complexities}
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{SGD}}(\varepsilon)
 &=O\!\left(1+\frac{L\Delta_0}{\varepsilon^2}
              +\frac{L\Delta_0\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^4}\right),\\
N_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(\varepsilon)
 &=O\!\left(1+\frac{\sigma^2+L\Delta_0}{\varepsilon^2}
              +\frac{\delta^2\Delta_0^2}{\varepsilon^4}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These are sufficient budgets, not lower bounds.
At a common budget $N\geq\max\{1,B_0\}$, the corresponding horizons
are $K_{\mathrm{SGD}}=N$ and
$K_{\mathrm{TSGD}}=1+\lfloor(N-B_0)/2\rfloor$.
Initialization accuracy and cost must therefore both enter the comparison.

\subsection{Why Refreshing Can Improve Complexity}
\label{app:common-class-lower-bound}

When BV and centered similarity hold together, unbiasedness gives
\[
\mathbb E[\|h(x,\xi)-h(y,\xi)\|^2]
=\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|^2+
\mathbb E[\|r(x,\xi)-r(y,\xi)\|^2]
\leq(L^2+\delta^2)\|x-y\|^2.
\]
The mean-squared-smoothness guarantees of SPIDER and PAGE therefore
give $O(\varepsilon^{-3})$ complexity on this same class
\citep{fang2018spider,li2021page}, with shared-sample access.
The target is $\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(\widehat x)\|]\leq\varepsilon$;
our squared-norm target implies it by Jensen's inequality.

\begin{corollary}[Fradin et al., Theorem 3.2, specialized]
\label{cor:common-class-lower-bound}
Fix positive bounds $\Delta,L,\sigma,\delta$ on the initial gap,
smoothness, BV, and centered similarity. For sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$, zero-respecting algorithms require
$\Omega(\Delta\delta\sigma/\varepsilon^3)$ evaluations in the worst
case to achieve either target above. The oracle allows any fixed
finite number of simultaneous shared-sample queries per round,
with independent samples across rounds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set $p=q=2$, $L_1=L$, and $\sigma_1=\sigma$ in
\citet[Theorem~3.2]{pmlr-v300-fradin26a}. Their moment and similarity
conditions become BV and centered similarity, and the bound is
\[
\Omega\!\left(\min\left\{
\frac{L\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}+\frac{L\Delta\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^4},
\frac{\sigma^2+(L+\delta)\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{\delta\Delta\sigma}{\varepsilon^3}\right\}\right).
\]
For fixed positive parameters and sufficiently small $\varepsilon$,
the second branch is smaller and its cubic term dominates.
The theorem's restriction $\varepsilon\leq c_1\sqrt{L\Delta}$ is then
satisfied. Each query round requires at least one evaluation;
Jensen's inequality extends the lower bound to the squared-norm target.
\end{proof}
Zero-respecting algorithms introduce only coordinates revealed by past
oracle responses when started at the origin; this is not a lower bound
for all randomized algorithms. It establishes optimality of the exponent
three for this class, not an $\varepsilon^{-4}$ lower bound for pure TSGD.
