\section{Oracle Complexity and Additional Algorithmic Details}
\label{app:oracle-complexity}

\subsection{Initialization and Accuracy Targets}
\label{app:initialization-cost}

Suppose an unbiased oracle at $x_0$ has variance at most $\sigma_0^2$.
With $B_0$ independent samples, independent also of subsequent
transport samples, set
\[
g_0=\frac1{B_0}\sum_{i=1}^{B_0}h(x_0,\xi_i^{(0)}).
\]
Independence and centering cancel the cross terms, giving
\[
E_0=\mathbb E[\|g_0-\nabla f(x_0)\|^2]
\leq\frac{\sigma_0^2}{B_0}.
\]
Thus
$B_0\geq\max\{1,\lceil3\sigma_0^2/\varepsilon^2\rceil\}$
ensures $E_0\leq\varepsilon^2/3$, as required by
Corollary~\ref{cor:tsgd-iteration-complexity}. Only the variance at
$x_0$ is needed for this construction. The main theorem itself
requires neither unbiased initialization nor a pointwise oracle.

Count $K$ iterate updates. Single-sample SGD uses $K$ pointwise
gradient evaluations, whereas the shared-sample TSGD construction
uses at most $B_0+2(K-1)$. Its final correction $D_K$ is unused:
$g_{K-1}$ suffices to compute $x_K$, and the output is selected from
$x_0,\ldots,x_{K-1}$. Under the common BV assumption, one can take
$\sigma_0^2\leq\sigma^2$. Combining the batch size above with
\eqref{eq:tsgd-iteration-complexity} and
\eqref{eq:sgd-iteration-complexity} gives sufficient evaluation
budgets of order
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:comparison-oracle-complexities}
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{SGD}}(\varepsilon)
 &=O\!\left(1+\frac{L\Delta_0}{\varepsilon^2}
              +\frac{L\Delta_0\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^4}\right),\\
N_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(\varepsilon)
 &=O\!\left(1+\frac{\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^2}
              +\frac{L\Delta_0}{\varepsilon^2}
              +\frac{\delta^2\Delta_0^2}{\varepsilon^4}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These are upper bounds for the stated implementations, not oracle
lower bounds. The initialization term depends on this choice of
$g_0$ and can change when other initial gradient information is
available.

\subsection{Comparing Bounds at Equal Iteration Counts}
\label{subsec:noise-mechanisms}

Suppose both BV and global $\delta$-similarity hold for the same
oracle, both methods start at the same deterministic $x_0$, and
$\Delta_0>0$. Define the exact optimized bounds
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{\mathrm{SGD}}(K)
 &:=\frac{\Delta_0}{K}
   \left(L+\sqrt{L^2+\frac{2KL\sigma^2}{\Delta_0}}\right),\\
U_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(K)
 &:=\frac{\Delta_0}{K}
   \left(L+\sqrt{L^2+4(K-1)\delta^2}\right)+E_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Subtracting shows that $U_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(K)<U_{\mathrm{SGD}}(K)$
holds exactly when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:optimized-certificate-comparison}
E_0<\frac{\Delta_0}{K}
\left[\sqrt{L^2+\frac{2KL\sigma^2}{\Delta_0}}
      -\sqrt{L^2+4(K-1)\delta^2}\right].
\end{equation}
With exact initialization this reduces to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:equal-horizon-comparison}
2(K-1)\delta^2\Delta_0<KL\sigma^2.
\end{equation}
For fixed positive $\delta,\sigma,\Delta_0$ and
$E_0=o(K^{-1/2})$, the leading terms favor the TSGD bound when
$2\delta^2\Delta_0<L\sigma^2$. In contrast, a fixed positive
$E_0$ remains in the TSGD bound while the tuned SGD bound tends to
zero. These inequalities compare upper bounds; they do not imply
the same ordering of actual stationarity errors.

The powers of the stepsize in intermediate calculations should not
be compared directly. For SGD, the one-step descent penalty is
$L\eta^2\sigma^2/2$; summing and dividing by the descent coefficient
gives $L\eta\sigma^2/(2-L\eta)$ in \eqref{eq:sgd-bv-bound}.
For TSGD, $\delta^2\eta^2M_k$ instead bounds the increase
$E_{k+1}-E_k$ in tracking error. This increment persists in subsequent
estimates and receives the weight $K-1-k$ in
\eqref{eq:weighted-error-accumulation}. The optimized bounds account
for this accumulation.

There is also a fixed-step comparison when $\delta=0$: TSGD may use
$\eta=1/L$ for all horizons, obtaining
$2L\Delta_0/K+E_0$, while SGD's corresponding bound is
$2L\Delta_0/K+\sigma^2$. For $\delta>0$, the sufficient condition
of Theorem~\ref{thm:tsgd-stationarity} makes the prescribed TSGD step
horizon dependent. This does not imply that every fixed-step TSGD
run must fail.

\subsection{Comparing Bounds at Equal Oracle Budgets}
\label{subsec:fair-oracle-comparison}

Let $B_0$ denote the cost of a chosen initialization with error $E_0$,
without imposing a batching or unbiasedness requirement. With the
cost convention above, an integer budget
$N\geq\max\{1,B_0\}$ allows
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:equal-budget-horizons}
K_{\mathrm{SGD}}=N,\qquad
K_{\mathrm{TSGD}}=1+\left\lfloor\frac{N-B_0}{2}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
The bounds to compare are therefore $U_{\mathrm{SGD}}(N)$ and
$U_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(K_{\mathrm{TSGD}})$, including the error of the
chosen initialization in the latter.

For fixed positive $\delta,\sigma,\Delta_0$, suppose
$B_0=o(N)$ and $E_0=o(N^{-1/2})$. Then
$K_{\mathrm{TSGD}}\sim N/2$ and
\[
U_{\mathrm{SGD}}(N)
\sim\frac{\sqrt{2L\Delta_0\sigma^2}}{\sqrt N},
\qquad
U_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(K_{\mathrm{TSGD}})
\sim\frac{2\sqrt2\delta\Delta_0}{\sqrt N}.
\]
The leading TSGD bound is smaller when
$4\delta^2\Delta_0<L\sigma^2$, compared with the equal-iteration
threshold $2\delta^2\Delta_0<L\sigma^2$. A fixed positive
initialization error does not satisfy these asymptotic conditions.
Exact initialization must also have its cost counted in $B_0$.
Direct increment oracles and implementations that evaluate endpoints
jointly require their own cost accounting.

\subsection{Refreshing Improves Complexity on a Common Problem Class}
\label{app:common-class-lower-bound}

\paragraph{Common assumptions and accuracy convention.}
Fix positive bounds $\Delta,L,\sigma,\delta$, and consider lower-bounded
objectives with initial gap at most $\Delta$ and $L$-Lipschitz gradients,
together with unbiased stochastic gradient oracles satisfying both BV
and centered $\delta$-similarity. These assumptions control individual
gradients and their differences simultaneously. Indeed, unbiasedness gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:similarity-implies-mean-square-smoothness}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\|h(x,\xi)-h(y,\xi)\|^2]
&=\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|^2
  +\mathbb E[\|r(x,\xi)-r(y,\xi)\|^2]\\
&\leq(L^2+\delta^2)\|x-y\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus the oracle is mean-squared smooth with constant
$\bar L=\sqrt{L^2+\delta^2}$.
We compare the number of stochastic-gradient evaluations needed to find
$\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(\widehat x)\|]\leq\varepsilon$, the criterion used
by \citet{arjevani2022lowerboundsnonconvexstochastic}, SPIDER, and PAGE.
Corollary~\ref{cor:tsgd-iteration-complexity} uses the stronger criterion
$\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(\widehat x)\|^2]\leq\varepsilon^2$, which implies
it by Jensen's inequality. The accuracy parameter is therefore on the
same gradient-norm scale, and the lower bound below applies to both
criteria. The oracle model allows gradients to be evaluated at multiple
points using the same sample, which is precisely the access TSGD uses
to form its differences.

\paragraph{Applicable upper bounds.}
For fixed positive parameter bounds,
\eqref{eq:comparison-oracle-complexities} gives
$O(\varepsilon^{-4})$ oracle budgets for SGD and pure TSGD, including
initialization. Mean-squared smoothness and BV also ensure that the
online guarantees of SPIDER \citep{fang2018spider} and PAGE
\citep{li2021page} apply, yielding $O(\varepsilon^{-3})$ complexity
with their prescribed batch sizes and refresh schedules.

\paragraph{A lower bound on the same class.}
The upper bounds show that $O(\varepsilon^{-3})$ evaluations suffice.
To justify optimality of this exponent, the lower bound must apply
with the same separately prescribed bounds $L$ and $\delta$.
Theorem~3.2 of \citet{pmlr-v300-fradin26a} provides such a bound for
zero-respecting algorithms. Starting at the origin, these algorithms
only introduce coordinates revealed by previous oracle responses.
This class includes the standard gradient-based implementations
discussed here, but does not include every randomized algorithm.

\begin{corollary}[Specialization of Fradin et al., Theorem 3.2]
\label{cor:common-class-lower-bound}
Fix $\Delta,L,\sigma,\delta>0$. For all sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$, any zero-respecting algorithm that guarantees
$\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(\widehat x)\|]\leq\varepsilon$ uniformly over
the class satisfying
Assumptions~\ref{ass:smooth-objective}--\ref{ass:oracle-similarity}
and $f(x_0)-f_{\inf}\leq\Delta$ requires
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:common-class-oracle-lower-bound}
\Omega\!\left(\frac{\Delta\delta\sigma}{\varepsilon^3}\right)
\end{equation}
stochastic-gradient evaluations in the worst case.
The oracle permits any fixed finite number of simultaneous query
points sharing the same sample, with independent samples across rounds.
The same lower bound holds for the stronger target
$\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(\widehat x)\|^2]\leq\varepsilon^2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Set $p=q=2$ in Theorem~3.2 of \citet{pmlr-v300-fradin26a}.
Their bounded central moment and $(q,\delta)$-similarity assumptions
then become BV and centered $\delta$-similarity, respectively.
With $L_1=L$ and $\sigma_1=\sigma$, their lower bound is
\[
\Omega\!\left(
\min\left\{
\frac{L\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{L\Delta\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^4},
\quad
\frac{\sigma^2+(L+\delta)\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{\delta\Delta\sigma}{\varepsilon^3}
\right\}
\right).
\]
The theorem applies for $0<\varepsilon\leq c_1\sqrt{L\Delta}$
with a universal $c_1>0$.
For fixed positive parameters, the first branch grows as
$L\Delta\sigma^2/\varepsilon^4$, whereas the second is asymptotic to
$\delta\Delta\sigma/\varepsilon^3$. Their ratio tends to infinity,
so the minimum is the second branch for sufficiently small
$\varepsilon$, giving the claimed bound.
The lower bound counts shared-sample query rounds, each requiring
at least one gradient evaluation, so it also bounds the evaluation
count. Finally,
$\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(\widehat x)\|]
\leq\sqrt{\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(\widehat x)\|^2]}$
extends the bound to the squared-norm criterion.
\end{proof}

Together with the SPIDER/PAGE upper bounds,
Corollary~\ref{cor:common-class-lower-bound} establishes optimality
of the $\varepsilon^{-3}$ exponent among zero-respecting algorithms
on this common class as $\varepsilon\to0$, with the positive bounds
$\Delta,L,\sigma,\delta$ held fixed. This does not establish an
$\Omega(\varepsilon^{-4})$ lower bound for pure TSGD; its
$O(\varepsilon^{-4})$ guarantee need not be tight, and exact transport
can already yield faster convergence.
