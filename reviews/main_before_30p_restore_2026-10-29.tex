\begin{abstract}
Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) estimates gradients. Transported SGD
(TSGD) estimates their changes and uses them to update an initial gradient
estimate along the optimization trajectory. Estimating changes can be
much easier than estimating gradients: even when individual gradients
are highly noisy, shared additive noise cancels exactly from their
differences, giving exact transport. With exact initialization as well,
TSGD follows gradient descent exactly, regardless of the noise magnitude.
This known recursion appears in several variance-reduced methods; here
we study it directly as a fundamental estimation mechanism.
Its accuracy depends on two error sources: initialization error,
which persists, and transport errors, which accumulate. Centered
similarity controls the latter through noise variation rather than
pointwise noise magnitude. We give a clean, unified convergence analysis
that separates these contributions, and derive exact quadratic dynamics
that quantify how initialization and transport errors interact.
Simple examples and least-squares experiments identify regimes
favoring TSGD or SGD. These results clarify both the value of pure
transport and why modern optimizers combine it with fresh estimation.
\end{abstract}
