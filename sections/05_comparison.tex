\label{sec:sgd-tsgd-comparison}

\subsection{Comparing Assumptions}
\label{subsec:comparing-assumptions}

To compare noise assumptions on a common pointwise oracle, write
$h(x,\xi)=\nabla f(x)+r(x,\xi)$ with
$\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[r(x,\xi)]=0$. Bounded variance (BV),
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-variance}, imposes
$\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|r(x,\xi)\|^2]\leq\sigma^2$ uniformly in $x$.
The ABC condition instead bounds the oracle's second moment by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:abc-condition}
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|h(x,\xi)\|^2]
\leq 2A\bigl(f(x)-f_{\inf}\bigr)
     +B\|\nabla f(x)\|^2+C
\qquad\text{for all }x,
\end{equation}
for finite constants $A,B,C\geq0$ \citep{khaled2023better}.
It permits the second moment to grow with objective suboptimality or
the gradient norm. Under unbiasedness,
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|h(x,\xi)\|^2]
=\|\nabla f(x)\|^2
 +\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|r(x,\xi)\|^2],
\]
so BV implies ABC with $(A,B,C)=(0,1,\sigma^2)$.

By contrast, the all-pairs form of $\delta$-similarity in
\eqref{eq:oracle-similarity} controls
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}
  [\|r(x,\xi)-r(y,\xi)\|^2]
\leq\delta^2\|x-y\|^2.
\]
It measures variation of \emph{coupled} gradient noise, not its
pointwise magnitude. This global oracle condition is sufficient for
Assumption~\ref{ass:centered-similarity}; the conditional
trajectory-level assumption used by TSGD can be weaker.

The inclusions stop at BV $\Rightarrow$ ABC. For example, with
$f(x)=x^2/2$ and $h(x,\xi)=(1+\xi)x$, where $\xi$ is centered with
variance $v>0$, ABC holds with $(A,B,C)=(0,1+v,0)$, but BV fails
because the noise variance is $vx^2$. Thus ABC is strictly broader
than BV.

Neither ABC nor BV implies global similarity. Take
$f(x)=x^2/2$, so $\nabla f(x)=x$, and let
$h(x,\xi)=x+\xi\sin(x^2)$ with a Rademacher $\xi$.
The oracle is unbiased and its noise variance is
$\mathbb E_{\xi}[|h(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x)|^2]=\sin^2(x^2)\leq1$.
Hence BV holds with $\sigma^2=1$, and ABC follows with
$(A,B,C)=(0,1,1)$. For a shared sample at $x$ and $y$, however,
the centered difference error has second moment
\[
\mathbb E_{\xi}[|r(x,\xi)-r(y,\xi)|^2]
=\bigl(\sin(x^2)-\sin(y^2)\bigr)^2.
\]
If \eqref{eq:oracle-similarity} held for a finite $\delta$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\sin(x^2)-\sin(y^2)|
&\leq\delta|x-y| &&\text{for all }x,y,\\
|2x\cos(x^2)|
&=\lim_{y\to x}
  \frac{|\sin(y^2)-\sin(x^2)|}{|y-x|}
\leq\delta &&\text{for all }x.
\end{aligned}
\]
But for $x_n=\sqrt{2\pi n}$,
$|2x_n\cos(x_n^2)|=2\sqrt{2\pi n}\to\infty$, a contradiction.

Conversely, global similarity need not imply even ABC. Take
$f(x)=\log\cosh x$, which is lower bounded and $1$-smooth, and
$h(x,\xi)=\tanh x+\xi x$, where $\mathbb E[\xi]=0$ and
$\mathbb E[\xi^2]=\delta^2>0$. Since $\nabla f(x)=\tanh x$,
the oracle is unbiased. Its centered noise satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_{\xi}[|r(x,\xi)-r(y,\xi)|^2]
=\delta^2(x-y)^2,
\]
so \eqref{eq:oracle-similarity} holds with equality. But the
pointwise noise variance $\delta^2x^2$ is unbounded, ruling out BV.
Moreover,
$\mathbb E_{\xi}[|h(x,\xi)|^2]=\tanh^2x+\delta^2x^2$,
while the ABC right-hand side obeys
\[
2A\log\cosh x+B\tanh^2x+C
\leq 2A|x|+B+C.
\]
For any finite $A,B,C$, the quadratic term $\delta^2x^2$ cannot be
bounded by this linear expression for all $x$. Thus similarity
does not imply ABC. These examples compare assumptions, not the
performance of SGD and TSGD on the respective objectives.
