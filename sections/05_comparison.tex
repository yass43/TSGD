\section{TSGD versus SGD: When Does Each Shine?}
\label{sec:sgd-tsgd-comparison}

We compare SGD and TSGD on the same objective, from the same initial
point, using a common unbiased pointwise oracle. This makes both
gradient noise and its coupling across points explicit. We first
compare assumptions and convergence bounds, then examine exact
trajectories that favor each method, and finally account for oracle
costs. Throughout, performance means the average expected squared
norm of the true gradient; an accurate initial estimate for TSGD is
an additional resource whose cost must be counted.

\subsection{Comparing Assumptions}
\label{subsec:comparing-assumptions}

To compare noise assumptions on a common pointwise oracle, write
$h(x,\xi)=\nabla f(x)+r(x,\xi)$ with
$\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[r(x,\xi)]=0$. Bounded variance (BV),
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-variance}, imposes
$\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|r(x,\xi)\|^2]\leq\sigma^2$ uniformly in $x$.
By contrast, the all-pairs form of $\delta$-similarity in
\eqref{eq:oracle-similarity} controls
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}
  [\|r(x,\xi)-r(y,\xi)\|^2]
\leq\delta^2\|x-y\|^2.
\]
It measures variation of \emph{coupled} gradient noise, not its
pointwise magnitude. This global oracle condition is sufficient for
Assumption~\ref{ass:centered-similarity}; the conditional
trajectory-level assumption used by TSGD can be weaker.

BV does not imply global similarity. Take
$f(x)=x^2/2$, so $\nabla f(x)=x$, and let
$h(x,\xi)=x+\xi\sin(x^2)$ with a Rademacher $\xi$.
The oracle is unbiased and its noise variance is
$\mathbb E_{\xi}[|h(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x)|^2]=\sin^2(x^2)\leq1$.
Hence BV holds with $\sigma^2=1$. For a shared sample at $x$ and $y$,
however, the centered difference error has second moment
\[
\mathbb E_{\xi}[|r(x,\xi)-r(y,\xi)|^2]
=\bigl(\sin(x^2)-\sin(y^2)\bigr)^2.
\]
If \eqref{eq:oracle-similarity} held for a finite $\delta$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\sin(x^2)-\sin(y^2)|
&\leq\delta|x-y| &&\text{for all }x,y,\\
|2x\cos(x^2)|
&=\lim_{y\to x}
  \frac{|\sin(y^2)-\sin(x^2)|}{|y-x|}
\leq\delta &&\text{for all }x.
\end{aligned}
\]
But for $x_n=\sqrt{2\pi n}$,
$|2x_n\cos(x_n^2)|=2\sqrt{2\pi n}\to\infty$, a contradiction.

Conversely, global similarity need not imply BV. Take
$f(x)=\log\cosh x$, which is lower bounded and $1$-smooth, and
$h(x,\xi)=\tanh x+\xi x$, where $\mathbb E[\xi]=0$ and
$\mathbb E[\xi^2]=\delta^2>0$. Since $\nabla f(x)=\tanh x$,
the oracle is unbiased. Its centered noise satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_{\xi}[|r(x,\xi)-r(y,\xi)|^2]
=\delta^2(x-y)^2,
\]
so \eqref{eq:oracle-similarity} holds with equality. But the
pointwise noise variance $\delta^2x^2$ is unbounded, ruling out BV.
Thus neither BV nor global similarity implies the other. These
examples compare assumptions, not the performance of SGD and TSGD
on the respective objectives.

\subsection{SGD Guarantees}
\label{subsec:sgd-guarantees}

Consider the SGD run \eqref{eq:sgd-update}--\eqref{eq:sgd-estimate}
under Assumptions~\ref{ass:smooth-objective}--\ref{ass:bounded-variance},
with a stepsize $\eta$ held constant for $K$ iterations. In this
subsection, $F_k$ and $G_k$ refer to the SGD iterates, and we write
$\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}:=K^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}G_k$.
Smoothness, unbiasedness, and BV give
\[
F_{k+1}\leq F_k-\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)G_k
                   +\frac{L\eta^2\sigma^2}{2}.
\]
Summing over $k=0,\ldots,K-1$ and using $F_K\geq0$ gives, for
$0<\eta<2/L$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sgd-bv-bound}
\boxed{\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}
\leq\frac{2\Delta_0+KL\eta^2\sigma^2}
          {\eta K(2-L\eta)}.}
\end{equation}
This is the standard nonconvex SGD guarantee under BV
\citep{ghadimi2013stochastic}.

\paragraph{The fixed step $\eta=1/L$.}
The same stepsize used by gradient descent yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sgd-bv-fixed-step}
\boxed{\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}
\leq\frac{2L\Delta_0}{K}+\sigma^2.}
\end{equation}
For $\sigma^2>0$, this bound has a nonvanishing noise term, even
though its optimization term decays as $K^{-1}$.

\paragraph{A horizon-dependent step.}
For $\Delta_0>0$, differentiating the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:sgd-bv-bound} shows that its unique minimizer in
$(0,2/L)$ solves
$L\sigma^2\eta^2+(2L\Delta_0/K)\eta-2\Delta_0/K=0$.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sgd-bv-optimal-step}
\boxed{\eta_{\mathrm{BV},K}^*
=\frac{2}{L+\sqrt{L^2+2KL\sigma^2/\Delta_0}}}.
\end{equation}
Substituting this step into \eqref{eq:sgd-bv-bound} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sgd-bv-optimized-bound}
\boxed{\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}
\leq\frac{\Delta_0}{K}
\left(L+\sqrt{L^2+\frac{2KL\sigma^2}{\Delta_0}}\right)
\leq\frac{2L\Delta_0}{K}
     +\sqrt{\frac{2L\Delta_0\sigma^2}{K}}.}
\end{equation}
The second inequality uses $\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt a+\sqrt b$.
When $\sigma^2=0$, \eqref{eq:sgd-bv-optimal-step} gives
$\eta_{\mathrm{BV},K}^*=1/L$ and recovers the deterministic bound
$2L\Delta_0/K$. For $\sigma^2>0$, the tuned step decreases with
$K$, removing the constant term in \eqref{eq:sgd-bv-fixed-step}
and yielding an $O(K^{-1/2})$ variance contribution.

These are constant-stepsize, finite-horizon guarantees for the same
average stationarity criterion as Theorem~\ref{thm:tsgd-stationarity}.
SGD's bound depends on pointwise noise variance $\sigma^2$; by
contrast, \eqref{eq:tsgd-simplified-bound} depends on transport
similarity $\delta$ and initialization error $E_0$. Their assumptions
and oracle costs differ, so the rates alone do not establish
universal dominance.

\subsection{Noise Mechanisms and Convergence Bounds}
\label{subsec:noise-mechanisms}

The same oracle noise $r(x,\xi)$ enters the two algorithms differently.
SGD samples $r(x_k,\xi_{k+1})$ afresh at each step; TSGD transports
its current estimate using the coupled difference
$r(x_{k+1},\xi_{k+1})-r(x_k,\xi_{k+1})$. The former depends on the
pointwise noise magnitude, while the latter depends on how the noise
changes between the two endpoints. Fresh sampling can replace an
inaccurate gradient estimate; pure transport retains its initial
error and adds the subsequent difference errors.

The powers of the stepsize in these two mechanisms require care.
For SGD, the noise term in the one-step descent inequality is
$L\eta^2\sigma^2/2$. Summing and dividing by the descent coefficient
gives the contribution $L\eta\sigma^2/(2-L\eta)$ in
\eqref{eq:sgd-bv-bound}. For TSGD, similarity instead bounds each
\emph{increase in tracking-error variance} by
\[
E_{k+1}-E_k\leq\delta^2\eta^2M_k,
\qquad
E_k\leq E_0+\delta^2\eta^2\sum_{t<k}M_t.
\]
The factor $\eta^2$ controls a single transport increment; its error
persists in later estimates. Comparing it directly with SGD's
$O(\eta\sigma^2)$ term would therefore compare different quantities.
The accumulated errors are already accounted for in the convergence
bounds below.

Suppose, for the moment, that both BV and $\delta$-similarity hold for
the same oracle and that both algorithms start at the same $x_0$.
Write $\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}$ for TSGD's average squared
gradient. The optimized bounds \eqref{eq:sgd-bv-optimized-bound} and
\eqref{eq:tsgd-optimized-bound} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{\mathrm{SGD}}(K)
&:=\frac{\Delta_0}{K}
   \left(L+\sqrt{L^2+\frac{2KL\sigma^2}{\Delta_0}}\right),\\
U_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(K)
&:=\frac{\Delta_0}{K}
   \left(L+\sqrt{L^2+4(K-1)\delta^2}\right)+E_0,
\end{aligned}
\]
for $\Delta_0>0$. Subtracting them shows that TSGD's upper bound is
strictly smaller precisely when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:optimized-certificate-comparison}
\boxed{E_0<\frac{\Delta_0}{K}
\left[\sqrt{L^2+\frac{2KL\sigma^2}{\Delta_0}}
      -\sqrt{L^2+4(K-1)\delta^2}\right].}
\end{equation}
With exact initialization, this reduces to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:equal-horizon-comparison}
2(K-1)\delta^2\Delta_0<KL\sigma^2.
\end{equation}
For fixed positive $\delta,\sigma,\Delta_0$ and initialization error
$E_0=o(K^{-1/2})$, the leading terms favor the TSGD bound when
$2\delta^2\Delta_0<L\sigma^2$. With a fixed $E_0>0$, however,
the TSGD bound tends to $E_0$, whereas the tuned SGD bound tends to
zero. These statements compare upper bounds at equal iteration
counts; they do not establish an ordering of the actual errors.
Section~\ref{subsec:fair-oracle-comparison} accounts for their
different oracle costs.

There is also a fixed-step contrast. If $\delta=0$, TSGD may use
$\eta=1/L$ for every horizon and has the bound
$\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}\leq2L\Delta_0/K+E_0$, against
$2L\Delta_0/K+\sigma^2$ for SGD by
\eqref{eq:sgd-bv-fixed-step}. For $\delta>0$, the sufficient
condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:tsgd-stationarity} instead makes its
admissible step horizon-dependent; this does not assert that every
fixed-step TSGD run must fail. The next two examples determine the
actual trajectories and stationarity errors.

\subsection{Additive Noise}
\label{subsec:additive-noise-comparison}

Consider the common scalar oracle
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:additive-comparison-oracle}
f(x)=\frac{x^2}{2},\qquad
h(x,\xi)=x+\xi,\qquad
\mathbb P(\xi=1)=\mathbb P(\xi=-1)=\frac12,\qquad x_0=1.
\end{equation}
Here $L=1$, $\Delta_0=1/2$, and BV holds with $\sigma^2=1$.
The noise is independent of $x$, so a shared-sample difference is
exact: $h(x,\xi)-h(y,\xi)=x-y$, and $\delta=0$.

For SGD with $\eta=1/L=1$, the update is
$x_{k+1}=-\xi_{k+1}$. Hence $G_k^{\mathrm{SGD}}=1$ for every
$k\geq0$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:additive-sgd-fixed}
\boxed{\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}=1.}
\end{equation}
The nonvanishing $\sigma^2$ term in \eqref{eq:sgd-bv-fixed-step}
is therefore a genuine feature of this fixed-step run, not just an
artifact of the bound.

With the horizon-dependent step from \eqref{eq:sgd-bv-optimal-step},
$\eta_K=2/(1+\sqrt{1+4K})$, set $r_K=1-\eta_K$. The SGD update is
$x_{k+1}=r_Kx_k-\eta_K\xi_{k+1}$. Since the fresh
$\xi_{k+1}$ is independent of $x_k$, centered, and has second
moment one, expanding the square gives
\[
G_{k+1}^{\mathrm{SGD}}
=\mathbb E[(r_Kx_k-\eta_K\xi_{k+1})^2]
=r_K^2G_k^{\mathrm{SGD}}+\eta_K^2.
\]
Solving this recursion from $G_0^{\mathrm{SGD}}=1$ yields
\[
G_k^{\mathrm{SGD}}=r_K^{2k}
  +\frac{\eta_K}{2-\eta_K}(1-r_K^{2k}).
\]
In particular, averaging the geometric terms gives
\[
\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}
=\frac{\eta_K}{2-\eta_K}
 +\left(1-\frac{\eta_K}{2-\eta_K}\right)
  \frac{1-r_K^{2K}}{K(1-r_K^2)}.
\]
Since $\eta_K\sim K^{-1/2}$, we have
$r_K^{2K}\leq e^{-2K\eta_K}\to0$. Moreover,
$1-r_K^2=\eta_K(2-\eta_K)\sim2K^{-1/2}$ and
$1-\eta_K/(2-\eta_K)\to1$. Thus each term on the right is
asymptotic to $1/(2\sqrt K)$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:additive-sgd-tuned}
\boxed{\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}
=\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt K}.}
\end{equation}
Thus tuning the step removes the fixed-step residual. The optimized
BV bound is $(1+\sqrt{1+4K})/(2K)$ here, so both its $K^{-1/2}$
rate and its leading constant are attained by this run.

For TSGD, use the fixed step $\eta=1$ and allow arbitrary
square-integrable $g_0=1+e_0$, with
$E_0=\mathbb E[|e_0|^2]$. Exact transport gives
$D_{k+1}=x_{k+1}-x_k=-g_k$, so $g_1=0$ and
$x_k=-e_0$ for every $k\geq1$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:additive-tsgd-exact}
\boxed{\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}
=\frac1K+\left(1-\frac1K\right)E_0.}
\end{equation}
With exact initialization, TSGD reaches the minimizer after one
step despite the additive oracle noise. With inexact initialization,
its limiting stationarity error is $E_0$: cancellation during
transport cannot repair the initial estimate. In this example,
additive noise favors TSGD when initialization is sufficiently
accurate, while horizon-tuned SGD eventually improves on any fixed
$E_0>0$.

\subsection{Multiplicative Noise}
\label{subsec:multiplicative-noise-comparison}

Now use the oracle
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:multiplicative-comparison-oracle}
f(x)=\frac{x^2}{2},\qquad
h(x,a)=a x,\qquad
\mathbb P(a=0)=\mathbb P(a=2)=\frac12,\qquad x_0=1.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathbb E[a]=1$ and $\mathbb E[(a-1)^2]=1$, the oracle is
unbiased, with $L=1$ and $r(x,a)=(a-1)x$. Since
$\mathbb E[|r(x,a)|^2]=x^2$, no finite global BV constant exists.
Nevertheless, coupled differences satisfy
$\mathbb E[|r(x,a)-r(y,a)|^2]=|x-y|^2$, so $\delta=1$.
The BV theorem of Section~\ref{subsec:sgd-guarantees} does not
apply here; the following comparison uses exact dynamics.

Give both algorithms the step $\eta=1/2$. For SGD,
$x_{k+1}=(1-a_{k+1}/2)x_k$: each sample either leaves the iterate
unchanged or sends it to the minimizer, where it remains. Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:multiplicative-sgd-exact}
\boxed{G_k^{\mathrm{SGD}}=2^{-k},\qquad
\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}
=\frac{2(1-2^{-K})}{K}.}
\end{equation}

For TSGD, initialize exactly with $g_0=\nabla f(x_0)=1$.
The shared-sample difference gives
$g_{k+1}=(1-a_{k+1}/2)g_k$. Let
$N:=\min\{n\geq1:a_n=2\}$, so $\mathbb P(N=n)=2^{-n}$.
Before the first such sample, $g_k=1$; afterward it is zero.
Therefore
\[
x_k=1-\frac12\min\{k,N\},\qquad
x_\infty=1-\frac N2.
\]
Since $\mathbb E[N]=2$ and $\mathbb E[N^2]=6$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:multiplicative-tsgd-residual}
\boxed{\lim_{k\to\infty}G_k^{\mathrm{TSGD}}
=\mathbb E\!\left[\left(1-\frac N2\right)^2\right]
=\frac12.}
\end{equation}
The estimator becomes zero almost surely in finite time, but the
limiting true gradient is nonzero with probability $3/4$. In
particular, $\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}\to1/2$, whereas
\eqref{eq:multiplicative-sgd-exact} decays as $K^{-1}$.

For completeness, the horizon-dependent TSGD step
\eqref{eq:tsgd-optimal-step} is
$\eta_K^\star=2/(1+\sqrt{4K-3})$. Here $L=\delta=1$,
$\Delta_0=1/2$, and $E_0=0$, so the optimized guarantee
\eqref{eq:tsgd-optimized-bound} becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:multiplicative-tsgd-tuned-bound}
\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}
\leq\frac{1/2}{K}\left(1+\sqrt{1+4(K-1)}\right)
=\frac{1+\sqrt{4K-3}}{2K}.
\end{equation}
The bound is asymptotically attained by the iterates themselves.
\begin{lemma}[Asymptotic sharpness for tuned TSGD]
\label{lem:multiplicative-tsgd-tuned-exact}
For the oracle \eqref{eq:multiplicative-comparison-oracle}, with
exact initialization and stepsize $\eta_K^\star$, the actual average
stationarity satisfies
\[
\lim_{K\to\infty}\sqrt K\,
\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}=1.
\]
\end{lemma}
The proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:proof-multiplicative-tsgd-tuned}.
For this prescribed choice of step, the TSGD bound therefore has the
correct leading rate and constant. With its fixed step $1/2$, SGD
has average stationarity error $\Theta(K^{-1})$, while TSGD with
$\eta_K^\star$ has error $\Theta(K^{-1/2})$. This is a comparison
of the stated runs, not a lower bound over all possible step
schedules. Fresh SGD samples become exact at the minimizer, while
transport can exhaust its estimator before reaching that minimizer.

\subsection{Similarity without Bounded Variance}
\label{subsec:similarity-without-bv}

The oracle $f(x)=\log\cosh x$ and
$h(x,\xi)=\tanh x+\xi x$ from
Section~\ref{subsec:comparing-assumptions} illustrates the scope of
the TSGD guarantee. Recall that $\mathbb E[\xi]=0$ and
$\mathbb E[\xi^2]=\delta^2>0$. The objective is $1$-smooth with
$f_{\inf}=0$, while the pointwise noise variance $\delta^2x^2$ is
unbounded. Nevertheless, the coupled increment
\[
D_{k+1}=\tanh x_{k+1}-\tanh x_k
        +\xi_{k+1}(x_{k+1}-x_k)
\]
satisfies the conditional similarity assumption with equality.
For deterministic $x_0$ and arbitrary initialization of finite
mean-square error, \eqref{eq:tsgd-optimized-bound} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:logcosh-tsgd-guarantee}
\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}
\leq\frac{\log\cosh x_0}{K}
       \left(1+\sqrt{1+4(K-1)\delta^2}\right)+E_0.
\end{equation}
With exact initialization, this tends to zero at rate
$O(K^{-1/2})$, although no global BV constant is available.

For comparison, SGD with a fixed $\eta>0$ has
$x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta\tanh x_k-\eta\xi_{k+1}x_k$.
Writing $V_k:=\mathbb E[x_k^2]$, conditional unbiasedness and
$|\tanh x|\leq1$ give
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{k+1}
&=\mathbb E[(x_k-\eta\tanh x_k)^2]+\eta^2\delta^2V_k\\
&\geq(1+\eta^2\delta^2)V_k-2\eta\sqrt{V_k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If $|x_0|\geq4/(\eta\delta^2)$, induction yields
$V_{k+1}\geq(1+\eta^2\delta^2/2)V_k$:
$2\eta\sqrt{V_k}\leq(\eta^2\delta^2/2)V_k$ whenever
$\sqrt{V_k}\geq4/(\eta\delta^2)$, and this growth preserves the
condition. This is divergence of the \emph{iterate second moment}.
It does not establish failure of stationarity, which here concerns
$\mathbb E[\tanh^2 x_k]$. The example demonstrates a TSGD guarantee
outside the global BV framework; failure of that assumption alone
does not imply that SGD cannot converge.

\subsection{Fixed Noise Magnitude, Variable Noise Variation}
\label{subsec:fixed-variance-variable-similarity}

The previous examples change the form of the noise. We now hold
its pointwise variance fixed while varying its spatial correlation.
For $L,\sigma>0$ and frequency $\omega\geq0$, consider
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fixed-variance-oracle}
f(x)=\frac L2x^2,\qquad
h(x,\varphi)=Lx+\sqrt2\sigma\cos(\omega x+\varphi),
\qquad \varphi\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,2\pi).
\end{equation}
Each update uses a fresh phase; the two evaluations in a TSGD
difference share that phase.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed variance and variable similarity]
\label{lem:fixed-variance-variable-similarity}
The noise $r(x,\varphi)=\sqrt2\sigma\cos(\omega x+\varphi)$ is
centered and satisfies, for every $x,y$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase-noise-moments}
\mathbb E_{\varphi}[r(x,\varphi)^2]=\sigma^2,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\varphi}[|r(x,\varphi)-r(y,\varphi)|^2]
=4\sigma^2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\omega(x-y)}2\right).
\end{equation}
Its smallest global similarity constant is $\delta=\sigma\omega$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A uniform phase makes the cosine have mean zero and second moment
$1/2$. For the difference, use
\[
r(x,\varphi)-r(y,\varphi)
=-2\sqrt2\sigma\sin\!\left(\frac{\omega(x+y)}2+\varphi\right)
                 \sin\!\left(\frac{\omega(x-y)}2\right).
\]
Squaring and averaging gives \eqref{eq:phase-noise-moments}.
The inequality $|\sin t|\leq|t|$ proves similarity with
$\delta=\sigma\omega$; this value is minimal because
\[
\lim_{y\to x}
\frac{4\sigma^2\sin^2(\omega(x-y)/2)}{(x-y)^2}
=\sigma^2\omega^2.
\]
\end{proof}

For SGD, the fresh noise is conditionally centered with variance
$\sigma^2$ at every iterate. Since $\nabla f(x)=Lx$, its exact
second-moment recursion is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase-sgd-dynamics}
G_{k+1}^{\mathrm{SGD}}
=(1-L\eta)^2G_k^{\mathrm{SGD}}+L^2\eta^2\sigma^2.
\end{equation}
For a common $x_0$ and stepsize, every frequency gives the same
sequence of expected squared gradient norms.

For TSGD, the martingale identity
\eqref{eq:transport-error-identity} instead gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase-tsgd-error}
\begin{aligned}
E_{k+1}-E_k
&=4\sigma^2\mathbb E\!\left[
\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\omega(x_{k+1}-x_k)}2\right)\right]\\
&\leq\sigma^2\omega^2\eta^2M_k.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
At $\omega=0$, the noise is additive and transport is exact.
For positive frequencies, its error depends on both the frequency
and the displacement. Thus identical pointwise variance and
identical SGD second-moment dynamics coexist with similarity
constants ranging from zero to arbitrarily large values.
The sine factor also shows why a large global $\delta$ need not
produce a large error on every step: the actual variation depends
on where the endpoints lie. Increasing the frequency need not
increase every realized transport error monotonically.

\subsection{Oracle Costs and When to Transport}
\label{subsec:fair-oracle-comparison}

An advantage at equal iteration counts need not be an advantage at
equal computational cost. In the shared point-oracle construction,
a fresh SGD estimate costs one evaluation, whereas a transported
difference costs two. Let $B_0$ be the number of point-oracle
evaluations used to construct a particular $g_0$, with resulting
error $E_0$. This notation imposes no batching or unbiasedness
requirement on initialization.

Counting $K$ iterate updates and omitting the unused final transport
correction, the costs are $K$ for SGD and $B_0+2(K-1)$ for TSGD.
An integer budget $N\geq\max\{1,B_0\}$ therefore allows
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:equal-budget-horizons}
K_{\mathrm{SGD}}=N,\qquad
K_{\mathrm{TSGD}}=1+\left\lfloor\frac{N-B_0}{2}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Under the common BV and similarity assumptions, the corresponding
comparison is between $U_{\mathrm{SGD}}(N)$ and
$U_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(K_{\mathrm{TSGD}})$, with the actual $E_0$
of the chosen initialization included in the latter.

For example, suppose $\delta,\sigma,\Delta_0>0$ are fixed,
$B_0=o(N)$, and $E_0=o(N^{-1/2})$. Then
$K_{\mathrm{TSGD}}\sim N/2$, and
\[
U_{\mathrm{SGD}}(N)
\sim\frac{\sqrt{2L\Delta_0\sigma^2}}{\sqrt N},\qquad
U_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(K_{\mathrm{TSGD}})
\sim\frac{2\sqrt2\delta\Delta_0}{\sqrt N}.
\]
The leading TSGD bound is smaller when
$4\delta^2\Delta_0<L\sigma^2$, rather than the equal-iteration
threshold $2\delta^2\Delta_0<L\sigma^2$ from
Section~\ref{subsec:noise-mechanisms}. Initialization with fixed
positive error does not meet these asymptotic conditions. If exact
initialization is supplied, its cost must still be included in
$B_0$. A direct increment oracle, or an implementation that evaluates
both endpoints jointly, requires its own cost accounting.

For $\Delta_0>0$ and an explicit accuracy target, let an independent
uniform output index $R$ select one of the iterates and require
$\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(x_R)\|^2]\leq\varepsilon^2$.
Under BV, \eqref{eq:sgd-bv-optimized-bound} shows that an integer
horizon satisfying
\[
K_{\mathrm{SGD}}\geq
\max\left\{1,\frac{4L\Delta_0}{\varepsilon^2},
              \frac{8L\Delta_0\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^4}\right\}
\]
is sufficient: each of the two terms in the simplified bound is at
most $\varepsilon^2/2$. For TSGD, the sufficient horizon is given
by \eqref{eq:tsgd-iteration-complexity}, provided
$E_0\leq\varepsilon^2/3$.

One possible way to obtain this initialization under the common BV
assumption is to average $B_0$ independent samples at $x_0$.
Then $E_0\leq\sigma^2/B_0$, so one may take
$B_0=\max\{1,\lceil3\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2\rceil\}$, as in
Section~\ref{subsec:tsgd-complexity}. The resulting sufficient
point-oracle evaluation budgets have orders
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:comparison-oracle-complexities}
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{SGD}}(\varepsilon)
&=O\!\left(1+\frac{L\Delta_0}{\varepsilon^2}
             +\frac{L\Delta_0\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^4}\right),\\
N_{\mathrm{TSGD}}(\varepsilon)
&=O\!\left(1+\frac{\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^2}
             +\frac{L\Delta_0}{\varepsilon^2}
             +\frac{\delta^2\Delta_0^2}{\varepsilon^4}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These are upper bounds obtained from the two convergence guarantees,
not oracle lower bounds. The initialization term in the TSGD budget
belongs to this particular construction of $g_0$; another source of
initial gradient information can change it.

The comparison depends on noise magnitude, noise variation, and
the cost of initialization. SGD benefits when fresh gradients are
accurate, especially when their noise vanishes near a minimizer.
TSGD benefits when a sufficiently accurate initial estimate can be
obtained and subsequent gradient differences have little noise.
The examples exhibit both possibilities, so neither method
universally dominates. Their complementary mechanisms motivate
the combinations of refreshing and transporting studied in
Section~6.
