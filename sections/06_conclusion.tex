\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Transported SGD is a fundamental way to use stochastic-gradient
information: it estimates changes and carries an existing estimate
along the trajectory. Its strength becomes visible when noise is
understood through its variation as well as its magnitude. Accurate
initialization and high similarity can make transport effective even
when individual gradients are very noisy. Its persistent initialization
error and accumulated transport errors explain both its limitations
and its complementarity with fresh estimation and momentum averaging.
Recognizing these distinct mechanisms clarifies why transport appears
throughout modern optimization, and why the balance between them matters.
