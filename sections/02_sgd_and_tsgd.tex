\section{SGD and TSGD---Algorithms and Error Mechanisms}
\label{sec:algorithms-and-errors}

\subsection{Algorithms and Assumptions}
\label{subsec:algorithms-and-assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Smooth, lower-bounded objective]
\label{ass:smooth-objective}
The differentiable function $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ is bounded below,
and its gradient is $L$-Lipschitz:
$\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|$ for all $x,y$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Unbiased stochastic gradient oracle]
\label{ass:unbiased-oracle}
The oracle $h(x,\xi)$ satisfies
$\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[h(x,\xi)]=\nabla f(x)$ for every $x$. Each sample used in
an update is fresh relative to the preceding iterates and estimates.
\end{assumption}

Starting from $x_0$ and using positive deterministic stepsizes $\eta_k$,
SGD has the iterate update and estimator rule
\begin{align}
x_{k+1}&=x_k-\eta_k g_k, \label{eq:sgd-update}\\
g_k&=h(x_k,\xi_{k+1}). \label{eq:sgd-estimate}
\end{align}
TSGD starts with an arbitrary gradient estimate $g_0$ and has the
corresponding rules
\begin{align}
x_{k+1}&=x_k-\eta_k g_k, \label{eq:tsgd-iterate}\\
g_{k+1}&=g_k+D_{k+1}. \label{eq:tsgd-estimate}
\end{align}
For the oracle in Assumption~\ref{ass:unbiased-oracle}, the transported
increment can be formed by evaluating the \emph{same} fresh sample at both
endpoints:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shared-sample-increment}
D_{k+1}
  =h(x_{k+1},\xi_{k+1})-h(x_k,\xi_{k+1}).
\end{equation}
The two algorithms describe separate runs. An SGD step uses one stochastic
gradient evaluation; the displayed TSGD transport step uses two. The
initial estimate $g_0$ may be random or biased; we assume only that
$\mathbb{E}[\|g_0-\nabla f(x_0)\|^2]<\infty$.

\begin{assumption}[Bounded variance]
\label{ass:bounded-variance}
The bounded-variance (BV) condition holds: for some $\sigma^2\geq0$,
the pointwise gradient noise satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bounded-variance}
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\bigl[\|h(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x)\|^2\bigr]
  \leq \sigma^2
\qquad\text{for every }x.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

For transport, let $\mathcal F_k$ contain all randomness available before
$D_{k+1}$ is sampled, including $x_{k+1}$ and $g_k$, and set
$m_k:=\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_k)$. The TSGD analysis uses the following
two conditions on the increments actually generated along the trajectory.

\begin{assumption}[Unbiased transport]
\label{ass:unbiased-transport}
The increment $D_{k+1}$ is conditionally unbiased:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unbiased-transport}
\mathbb{E}[D_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]=m_k.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Centered $\delta$-similarity]
\label{ass:centered-similarity}
For some $\delta\geq0$, the conditional mean-square transport error obeys
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:centered-similarity}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D_{k+1}-m_k\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k\bigr]
  \leq\delta^2\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|^2.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

Assumptions~\ref{ass:unbiased-oracle} and \ref{ass:bounded-variance}
describe the usual SGD oracle and its noise. TSGD needs
Assumptions~\ref{ass:unbiased-transport} and
\ref{ass:centered-similarity}, not a pointwise oracle or bounded variance.
For the shared-sample construction \eqref{eq:shared-sample-increment}, a
convenient sufficient condition for Assumption~\ref{ass:centered-similarity}
is the all-pairs oracle bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:oracle-similarity}
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[
\left\|
  \bigl[h(x,\xi)-h(y,\xi)\bigr]
  -\bigl[\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\bigr]
\right\|^2\right]
  \leq\delta^2\|x-y\|^2
\qquad\text{for all }x,y.
\end{equation}
The same $\xi$ appears at $x$ and $y$. More generally, the conditional
assumptions allow increments $D_{k+1}$ that do not come from two pointwise
oracle evaluations. We compare the noise conditions in Section~5.

\subsection{Understanding Similarity}
\label{subsec:understanding-similarity}

The parameter $\delta$ measures how accurately a stochastic difference
tracks the \emph{true gradient change}. It does not measure the size of the
gradient change itself. The subtraction in
\eqref{eq:oracle-similarity} is what allows $\delta$ to vanish even when
the true gradient changes substantially. Consequently, an objective with
substantial curvature can still have noiseless transport.

For a finite sum $f(x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f_i(x)$, consider quadratic
components
\[
f_i(x)=\tfrac12 x^\top A_i x+b_i^\top x+c_i,
\qquad A_i=A_i^\top,
\]
and sample $i$ uniformly, so that $h(x,i)=\nabla f_i(x)$. Write
$\bar A=\frac1n\sum_i A_i$. Then the centered difference in
\eqref{eq:oracle-similarity} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:quadratic-difference-noise}
\bigl[\nabla f_i(x)-\nabla f_i(y)\bigr]
-\bigl[\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\bigr]
  =(A_i-\bar A)(x-y).
\end{equation}
The linear terms $b_i$ disappear, however different they may be. The
smallest constant in the all-pairs bound \eqref{eq:oracle-similarity}
is therefore
\[
\delta_{\min}^2
  =\lambda_{\max}\!\left(
    \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(A_i-\bar A)^2
  \right),
\]
which measures variation among the component Hessians. By contrast, the
smoothness constant of the average quadratic is
$L=\|\bar A\|_{\mathrm{op}}$.

There is no fixed ordering between $\delta$ and $L$. If all $A_i$ coincide,
then $\delta_{\min}=0$ even when $L$ is large and the $b_i$ make individual
gradients very noisy. In the opposite direction, take $n\geq3$ scalar
components with $f_1(x)=\frac{nL}{2}x^2$ and $f_i(x)=0$ for $i\geq2$.
Their average is $f(x)=\frac L2x^2$, yet
$\delta_{\min}=L\sqrt{n-1}>L$. These examples use finite sums only to make
the distinction visible. What matters for transport is how closely a
\emph{stochastic gradient difference} matches the corresponding
\emph{deterministic gradient difference}. The parameter $\delta$ measures
that discrepancy per unit displacement, regardless of the objective's
structure.

\subsection{The Martingale Interpretation}
\label{subsec:martingale-interpretation}

Define the estimation error, its mean-square size, and the centered
transport error by
\[
e_k:=g_k-\nabla f(x_k),\qquad
E_k:=\mathbb{E}[\|e_k\|^2],\qquad
d_{k+1}:=D_{k+1}-m_k.
\]
The update \eqref{eq:tsgd-estimate} gives the exact recursion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:estimation-error-recursion}
e_{k+1}=e_k+d_{k+1},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[d_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]=0.
\end{equation}
Thus $(e_k)$ is a martingale, starting from whatever error is present in
$g_0$. Because $e_k$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal F_k$, the cross
term vanishes when we square \eqref{eq:estimation-error-recursion} and take
expectations. Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transport-error-identity}
E_{k+1}=E_k+\mathbb{E}[\|d_{k+1}\|^2],
\qquad
E_k=E_0+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}[\|d_{t+1}\|^2].
\end{equation}
Initialization error persists in this identity, while centered transport
errors add to its mean-square size. The similarity condition further gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transport-error-growth}
E_k\leq E_0+\delta^2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}
  \eta_t^2\mathbb{E}[\|g_t\|^2].
\end{equation}

There is also a descent interpretation. At step $k$, TSGD uses
$g_k=\nabla f(x_k)+e_k$, which is the gradient at $x_k$ of the linearly
tilted objective $x\mapsto f(x)+\langle e_k,x\rangle$. This viewpoint
interprets $e_k$ as the current tilt; the tilt changes with the
transport error $d_{k+1}$.

\subsection{Comparing the Error Mechanisms}
\label{subsec:comparing-error-mechanisms}

SGD draws a new gradient estimate at every step. Under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:unbiased-oracle} and \ref{ass:bounded-variance},
each estimate is unbiased given $x_k$ and has mean-square error at most
$\sigma^2$. The next estimate does not inherit the preceding estimator
error, although earlier noisy steps still affect the trajectory.

TSGD instead carries its estimate forward. Its error consists of the
initial error plus all subsequent transport errors, exactly as
\eqref{eq:transport-error-identity} shows. When $\delta$ and the step
lengths are small, each new transport error is locally small; the sum in
\eqref{eq:transport-error-growth} shows why controlling their accumulation
is the central task of the convergence analysis in Section~3. Accurate
initialization and small noise variation can favor TSGD, while accurate
fresh gradients can favor SGD when transport is noisy. Neither method
dominates in general. Section~5 develops the comparison in detail.
