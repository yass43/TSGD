\section{When Does TSGD Shine?}
\label{sec:when-tsgd-shines}

The distinction between noise magnitude and noise variation is easiest
to see on simple objectives. We compare the expected squared norm of the
true gradient, $G_k:=\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2]$, keeping track of the
initial estimate as a separate resource.

\subsection{Accurate Differences Despite Noisy Gradients}
\label{subsec:additive-noise-comparison}

Consider $f(x)=x^2/2$ and an unbiased oracle $h(x,\xi)=x+\xi$,
where $\mathbb E[\xi]=0$ and $\mathbb E[\xi^2]=\sigma^2$.
The individual gradients can be arbitrarily noisy, yet
\[
h(x,\xi)-h(y,\xi)=x-y=\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y).
\]
Thus $\delta=0$, independently of $\sigma$. With exact
initialization, TSGD follows gradient descent exactly. Indeed,
$g_k=\nabla f(x_k)$ and an exact difference give
\[
g_{k+1}=g_k+\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_k)=\nabla f(x_{k+1}).
\]
Induction from $g_0=\nabla f(x_0)$ proves this for any objective.
Appendix~\ref{subsec:exact-init-exact-transport} gives the resulting
GD convergence guarantee and proves that the gradients tend to zero.
For the present quadratic, $x_0=1$ and $\eta=1$ give
\[
G_k^{\mathrm{TSGD}}=0,\qquad
G_k^{\mathrm{SGD}}=\sigma^2,\qquad k\geq1.
\]
TSGD reaches the minimizer after one step, while SGD has
$x_{k+1}=-\xi_k$. Tuning SGD's stepsize to the horizon makes its
average stationarity error decay, but does not recover the noiseless
trajectory (Appendix~\ref{app:additive-details}). Exact transport
alone cannot compensate for an inaccurate initial estimate.

\subsection{Fixed Noise Magnitude, Variable Similarity}
\label{subsec:fixed-variance-variable-similarity}

To determine whether noise magnitude alone explains the relative behavior
of SGD and TSGD, we compare oracles with the same pointwise noise
distribution but different noise variation. For $\omega\geq0$, consider
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fixed-variance-oracle}
f(x)=\frac L2x^2,\qquad
h(x,\varphi)=Lx+\sqrt2\sigma\cos(\omega x+\varphi),
\qquad \varphi\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,2\pi).
\end{equation}
Each new sample is an independent phase; a transport difference
uses the same phase at both endpoints. For the noise
$r(x,\varphi)=h(x,\varphi)-Lx$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase-noise-moments}
\mathbb E[r(x,\varphi)^2]=\sigma^2,\qquad
\mathbb E[|r(x,\varphi)-r(y,\varphi)|^2]
=4\sigma^2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\omega(x-y)}2\right).
\end{equation}
The smallest global similarity constant is $\delta=\sigma\omega$
(Appendix~\ref{app:sinusoidal-proof}). Pointwise noise has the same
distribution for every $x$ and $\omega$, so SGD has identical
expected squared-gradient dynamics at every frequency. TSGD,
however, has exact transport at $\omega=0$, while its similarity
constant grows as $\delta=\sigma\omega$. Its actual
transport error depends on the displacement through the sine term.

\caption{Same pointwise variance, different transport accuracy.

Figure~\ref{fig:similarity-experiment} isolates this distinction at
equal oracle cost. At small frequencies, TSGD retains accurate
information despite the persistent pointwise noise. At $\omega=2$,
its mean squared true-gradient norm exceeds SGD's in these runs.
Thus, at the same pointwise noise level and stepsize, changing the
noise variation changes which method performs better.

\subsection{When Fresh Gradients Are Preferable}
\label{subsec:multiplicative-noise-comparison}

Now let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:multiplicative-comparison-oracle}
f(x)=\frac{x^2}{2},\qquad h(x,a)=ax,\qquad
\mathbb P(a=0)=\mathbb P(a=2)=\frac12,\qquad x_0=1.
\end{equation}
Here the gradient-noise variance is $x^2$: fresh gradients become
exact at the minimizer, although global BV fails. Similarity holds
with $\delta=1$. At the common stepsize $\eta=1/2$, each SGD sample
either leaves the iterate unchanged or sends it to zero, so
$G_k^{\mathrm{SGD}}=2^{-k}$. In contrast, exactly initialized TSGD
satisfies $g_{k+1}=(1-a_{k+1}/2)g_k$. Its estimator becomes zero at
the first sample $a_{k+1}=2$, wherever the iterate happens to be.
The exact calculation in Appendix~\ref{app:multiplicative-details}
gives
\[
g_k\longrightarrow0\quad\text{almost surely},\qquad
G_k^{\mathrm{TSGD}}\longrightarrow\frac12.
\]
This is false convergence: a vanishing estimate need not indicate
a vanishing true gradient. Fresh information can therefore be
preferable even when initialization is exact.

\subsection{What Transport Cannot Do}
\label{subsec:four-case-summary}

To separate the effects of initialization and transport, consider
$f(x)=\lambda x^2/2$ and $h(x;a,b)=(\lambda+a)x+b$, with $\lambda>0$,
independent noises satisfying $\mathbb E[a]=\mathbb E[b]=0$,
$\mathbb E[a^2]=\delta^2$, and $\mathbb E[b^2]<\infty$.
Shared sampling cancels $b$. Table~\ref{tab:four-error-regimes} summarizes
the four cases, whose exact dynamics are developed in
Appendix~\ref{app:quadratic-dynamics}.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Initialization & Exact transport & Stochastic transport\\
\midrule
Exact & Gradient descent & Accumulated transport error\\
Inexact & Persistent initial error & Both errors and their interaction\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Four regimes in the quadratic model, with a stable constant
stepsize. The exact-transport identities also hold for general
objectives; the explicit limiting errors and stability condition
are established in Appendix~\ref{app:quadratic-dynamics}.}
\label{tab:four-error-regimes}
\end{table}

\emph{Exact transport preserves initialization error.}
When $\delta=0$, the error identity gives $e_k=e_0$, hence
$g_k=\nabla f(x_k)+e_0$ for every $k$. With $e_0=0$ this is GD;
otherwise it is GD on $f(x)+\langle e_0,x\rangle$. The obstruction
is informational: adding a linear term leaves all gradient differences
unchanged, so differences alone cannot recover a missing constant
gradient offset. On the quadratic, for $0<\eta<2/\lambda$,
$x_k\to-e_0/\lambda$ in mean square and $G_k\to E_0$
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:inexact-init-exact-transport}).

\emph{Stochastic transport can produce a nonzero error floor.}
For $0<\eta<2\lambda/(\lambda^2+\delta^2)$ and
$M_0:=\mathbb E[|g_0|^2]$, the exact quadratic dynamics give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:quadratic-residual-overview}
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb E[|g_k|^2]=0,\qquad
\lim_{k\to\infty}G_k
=E_0+\frac{\eta\delta^2 M_0}{2\lambda-\eta(\lambda^2+\delta^2)}.
\end{equation}
With exact initialization, $M_0=\lambda^2x_0^2$: if $\delta>0$ and
$x_0\ne0$, the estimate vanishes in mean square while the true gradient
has a positive limiting mean-square norm.
Appendix~\ref{subsec:exact-init-stochastic-transport} proves both limits.

\emph{The two errors interact.} Initialization affects the residual
directly through $E_0$ and indirectly through $M_0$, which controls the
steps and the transport errors they generate. For centered initialization
error, $M_0=\lambda^2x_0^2+E_0$, so improving the initial estimate reduces
both contributions. Appendix~\ref{subsec:inexact-init-stochastic-transport}
derives this interaction and the associated conservation law.
