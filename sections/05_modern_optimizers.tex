SARAH and SPIDER use transport between gradient recomputations
\citep{nguyen2017sarah,nguyen2017sarah_nonconvex,fang2018spider};
Inexact SARAH allows batch initialization \citep{nguyen2021inexact},
and Geom-SARAH randomizes phase lengths \citep{horvath2022adaptivity}.
PAGE refreshes probabilistically \citep{li2021page}, while MARINA
transports compressed differences \citep{gorbunov2021marina}.
MVR/STORM averages fresh and transported estimates
\citep{cutkosky2019momentum}; unclipped MARS includes MSGD at
$\gamma=0$ and MVR at $\gamma=1$ \citep{yuan2025mars}.
These identities concern the estimators before clipping or
preconditioning; cached gradients need not satisfy the shared-sample
transport assumptions.

SVRG \citep{NIPS2013_ac1dd209} and SAGA
\citep{defazio2014sagafastincrementalgradient} also exploit gradient
differences, but do not transport an estimate along successive iterates.
SVRG uses an anchor point and its full gradient; SAGA uses stored
component gradients and their average. Their standard estimates are
conditionally unbiased, whereas TSGD inherits tracking error.
Gradient differences also appear in decentralized gradient tracking,
where agents combine local gradient changes with neighbors' information
to track the network's average gradient \citep{Qu_2018}.

\paragraph{Practical relevance.}
SARAH and SARAH+ were evaluated on MNIST and CIFAR-10 classifiers
\citep{nguyen2017sarah_nonconvex}, PAGE on LeNet, VGG, and ResNet
\citep{li2021page}, and MARS on GPT-2 language models
\citep{yuan2025mars}. These experiments use composite optimizers,
not uninterrupted TSGD: even the paper analyzing SARAH-IN uses
restarted SARAH/SARAH+ in its neural-network experiments.
Some practical MARS variants also use different samples in their
gradient corrections. These results do not establish pure TSGD's
performance in isolation.

\subsection{Why Combine Fundamental Estimators?}
\label{subsec:why-combine-estimators}

Modern optimizers combine fresh estimation, averaging, and transport
through weighted averages or refreshes. Three considerations motivate
these combinations.

\emph{Computational efficiency.} Accurate gradient changes may require
far fewer samples than accurate absolute gradients. Transport can then
preserve an expensive initial estimate through many cheaper steps,
despite using two evaluations per sample. More frequent refreshes limit
error accumulation but reduce this saving.

\emph{Complementary strengths.} Momentum averages past estimates to
reduce noise, but can lag behind a rapidly changing gradient. Transport
accounts for this change, while fresh gradients correct inherited and
accumulated errors at the cost of introducing pointwise noise.
MVR, STORM, and MARS combine these roles even in a fixed noise regime.

\emph{Changing regimes.} Shorter steps tighten the similarity bound on
new transport errors without removing old ones. Fresh gradients may
also improve: their noise vanishes near the minimizer in
Section~\ref{subsec:multiplicative-noise-comparison}, while transport
retains a residual. The relative reliability of the two information
sources can therefore change during a run. Choosing refresh frequencies
or mixing weights requires balancing their accuracy and cost, generally
without knowing their errors.

\paragraph{Improved complexity through refreshing.}
When BV and centered similarity hold together, SPIDER
\citep{fang2018spider} and PAGE \citep{li2021page} exploit both assumptions
by combining accurate gradient recomputations with cheaper transport.
Refreshes limit how long transport errors accumulate, allowing larger
steps without an expensive estimate at every iteration.
Their prescribed batch sizes and refresh schedules give
$O(\varepsilon^{-3})$ oracle complexity, compared with the
$O(\varepsilon^{-4})$ guarantees for SGD and pure TSGD in
Section~\ref{sec:tsgd-convergence}.
The exponent three matches the lower bound of
\citet{pmlr-v300-fradin26a} for zero-respecting algorithms on the same
class (Appendix~\ref{app:common-class-lower-bound}).
This comparison motivates combining estimators; it is not an
$\Omega(\varepsilon^{-4})$ lower bound for pure TSGD.
