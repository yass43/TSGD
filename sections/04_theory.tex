\section{Convergence Theory and Comparison with SGD}
\label{sec:tsgd-convergence}

The examples distinguish noise magnitude from noise variation. We now
make this distinction explicit in finite-horizon stationarity guarantees.
Using $G_k$ from the preceding section, write
\[
\Delta_0:=f(x_0)-f_{\inf},\qquad
\overline G_K:=\frac1K\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}G_k,
\]
with a superscript identifying the algorithm when needed.

\subsection{Main Convergence Guarantee for TSGD}
\label{subsec:main-tsgd-theorem}

Using the history $\mathcal F_k$ defined in
Section~\ref{subsec:martingale-interpretation}, write
$m_k:=\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_k)$. We impose two conditions
on the increments actually used along the trajectory.
\begin{assumption}[Unbiased transport]
\label{ass:unbiased-transport}
The increments satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unbiased-transport}
\mathbb E[D_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]=m_k.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Similarity along the trajectory]
\label{ass:centered-similarity}
For some $\delta\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:centered-similarity}
\mathbb E[\|D_{k+1}-m_k\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k]
\leq\delta^2\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|^2.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
Assumptions~\ref{ass:unbiased-oracle} and \ref{ass:oracle-similarity}
imply these two conditions for shared-sample gradient differences.
The conditional similarity requirement may be weaker than global
similarity because it concerns only the generated increments.
Stating them directly on the increments also allows transport without
a pointwise gradient oracle.

\begin{mdframed}[
  nobreak=true,
  linecolor=black!55,linewidth=0.7pt,
  innerleftmargin=10pt,innerrightmargin=10pt,
  innertopmargin=7pt,innerbottommargin=7pt
]
\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon stationarity of TSGD]
\label{thm:tsgd-stationarity}
Suppose the objective is smooth and lower bounded
(Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth-objective}), and the transport increments
are conditionally unbiased and satisfy centered $\delta$-similarity
(Assumptions~\ref{ass:unbiased-transport}--\ref{ass:centered-similarity}).
Allow any initial estimate with $E_0<\infty$, including biased ones.
For an integer $K\geq1$, choose a constant stepsize $\eta>0$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tsgd-step-condition}
L\eta+(K-1)\delta^2\eta^2\leq1.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tsgd-stationarity-bound}
\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}
\leq\frac{2\Delta_0}{\eta K}+E_0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\end{mdframed}

\paragraph{Why the guarantee holds.}
Let $F_k:=\mathbb E[f(x_k)-f_{\inf}]$ and
$M_k:=\mathbb E[\|g_k\|^2]$. Smoothness gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tsgd-fundamental-descent}
F_{k+1}\leq F_k-\frac\eta2G_k
  -\frac\eta2(1-L\eta)M_k+\frac\eta2E_k.
\end{equation}
The error identity \eqref{eq:transport-error-identity} and similarity
imply $E_k\leq E_0+\delta^2\eta^2\sum_{t<k}M_t$.
When the descent inequalities are summed, an error generated at step
$t$ enters at most $K-1-t$ later estimates. Condition
\eqref{eq:tsgd-step-condition} ensures that its total contribution is
no larger than the corresponding negative $M_t$ term. This leaves the
initialization error and the initial objective gap in
\eqref{eq:tsgd-stationarity-bound}. Appendix~\ref{app:convergence-proofs}
gives the full proof, retaining the terminal objective gap and all
estimator-norm terms. Neither bounded variance nor a pointwise
gradient oracle is required.

Choosing the largest admissible stepsize gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tsgd-simplified-bound}
\boxed{\overline G_K^{\mathrm{TSGD}}
\leq\frac{2L\Delta_0}{K}
    +\frac{2\delta\Delta_0}{\sqrt K}+E_0.}
\end{equation}
The TSGD bound does not explicitly depend on $\sigma$, but this
dependence may enter through $E_0$. For example, initializing $g_0$
by averaging $B_0$ independent unbiased stochastic gradients at $x_0$
gives $E_0\leq\sigma^2/B_0$ under bounded variance.
Figure~\ref{fig:ls-init} illustrates how a more accurate sampled initial
estimate lowers TSGD's residual error.
Thus, TSGD's sensitivity to gradient-noise magnitude depends on how
its initial estimate is constructed.

The terms correspond to deterministic optimization, accumulated
transport noise, and initialization error. Small $\delta$ permits larger
steps and a smaller transport contribution. With $\delta=0$, the
admissible step is $1/L$; if also $E_0=0$, we recover gradient descent.
The additive $E_0$ term reflects the error that pure transport preserves,
and its coefficient is sharp (Appendix~\ref{app:quadratic-dynamics}).
The guarantee approaches $E_0$ as the horizon grows. This floor can be
attained even with exact transport, as
Section~\ref{subsec:four-case-summary} shows. Stochastic transport can
create an additional floor when the stepsize is held fixed. For
$\delta>0$, the theorem controls this accumulation by choosing a
smaller stepsize for a longer run.

Related single-run guarantees appear in SARAH-IN, iSARAH-IN, and
PAGE-NS \citep{nguyen2017sarah_nonconvex,nguyen2021inexact,sokolov2022nonconvex}.
The formulation here expresses the argument directly through unbiased
increments and their centered similarity.

\subsection{Comparison with SGD}
\label{subsec:sgd-guarantees}

For SGD, unbiasedness and BV give the standard smooth nonconvex
guarantee \citep{ghadimi2013stochastic}. The fixed step $\eta=1/L$
yields $\overline G_K^{\mathrm{SGD}}\leq2L\Delta_0/K+\sigma^2$.
For $\Delta_0>0$, optimizing the constant step for the prescribed
horizon replaces this persistent variance term by a decaying one, as
shown in Table~\ref{tab:sgd-tsgd-guarantees}. The exact stepsizes and
derivations are in Appendix~\ref{app:convergence-proofs}.

The comparison reflects the different information the methods use:
SGD's bound depends on noise magnitude, while TSGD's depends on noise
variation and initial accuracy. Small $\delta$ can favor transport even
when $\sigma$ is large. Neither BV nor the more general ABC condition
is comparable with global similarity
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:comparing-assumptions}), so these bounds apply under
different assumptions and do not establish universal superiority.

Under smoothness and BV alone, SGD's $O(\varepsilon^{-4})$ oracle
complexity for $\mathbb E[\|\nabla f(\widehat x)\|]\leq\varepsilon$
is minimax optimal in its accuracy dependence
\citep{arjevani2022lowerboundsnonconvexstochastic}.
This does not establish optimality under additional similarity.
For TSGD, Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplicative-tsgd-tuned-exact} establishes
sharpness for the prescribed stepsize, not for all possible schedules.

\subsection{Computational Considerations}
\label{subsec:computational-considerations}

TSGD requires an initial estimate and, in the shared pointwise-oracle
implementation, two gradient evaluations per transport increment.
Accurate differences may nevertheless need far fewer samples than
accurate absolute gradients: small-batch transport can preserve
information obtained from a more expensive initial estimate. The
benefit depends on both its initial cost and the subsequent accuracy
of the differences. Iteration counts alone therefore do not compare
computational work. Appendix~\ref{app:oracle-complexity} gives batch
initialization costs and comparisons at equal oracle budgets.
